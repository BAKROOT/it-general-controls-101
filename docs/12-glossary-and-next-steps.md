## Where to go next

### Primary framework sources

Going straight to the source frameworks (rather than blog summaries) is the best way to deepen understanding:

- **COSO** — the *Internal Control — Integrated Framework*. The authoritative definition of internal control that SOX programs map to.
- **COBIT** — published by **ISACA**; the go-to for organizing IT governance and control objectives across the ITGC domains.
- **ITIL** — for the operations and change-enablement practices behind [computer operations](08-computer-operations.md) and [change management](06-change-management.md).
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework** — freely published by NIST; excellent for the security side of [access controls](05-access-to-programs-and-data.md).

Re-read [chapter 03](03-frameworks.md) with these sources open and the pieces will click into place.

### Certifications worth knowing

- **CISA (Certified Information Systems Auditor)** — from **ISACA**, the flagship credential for IT auditors and the most directly relevant to ITGC work.
- **CISM / CRISC** — also from ISACA, oriented toward security management and risk, respectively.
- **CIA (Certified Internal Auditor)** — from the IIA, broader internal-audit focus.
- General security certs (e.g., **Security+**) can help build the access/security foundation.

You don't need a certification to start learning or working, but CISA in particular signals credibility in this field.

### ISACA resources

**ISACA** is the professional body most associated with IT audit and governance. Its publications, glossaries, and communities are a natural next stop, and it maintains both COBIT and CISA.

## ITGC career paths

The skills in this repo open several doors:

- **IT Auditor** — testing ITGC (internal or external audit), the most direct path. Great entry point for the field.
- **GRC Analyst / Manager** — *Governance, Risk, and Compliance* roles that design, monitor, and coordinate controls (including ITGC) across an organization.
- **Controls / SOX Analyst** — focused on supporting the SOX program and management's assertion.
- **Security & Identity roles** — the access domain overlaps heavily with identity and access management (IAM) and broader cybersecurity.

A common journey: start as an IT auditor (often at an accounting/advisory firm), earn the CISA, then move into an in-house GRC or controls role — or specialize in security.

## A parting mental model

Come back to the one picture whenever ITGC feels abstract:

```
Financial statements   <- what the world relies on
Application controls   <- keep each transaction correct
IT General Controls    <- keep the systems themselves trustworthy
```

ITGC is the quiet foundation. Master it, and you understand why the numbers can be trusted.
